「让」/「叫」: make, let, tell someone to (causative)

HSK 2

A + 让／叫 + B + 動詞（+目的語）

"A makes/lets B do ..." is A + 「让」 + B + verb. Like English "make/let someone do," the verb itself doesn't change; you simply put 「让」 before the person. 「让」 has a broad range of meanings (make, let, ask), while 「叫」 is colloquial and has a stronger sense of "tell (order) someone to." The negative is 「不让」 (not let).

×「妈妈让我回家了让。」→ The verb stays in its ordinary form. 「让我」 + verb means "let me ..." and is often used for polite offers (「让我来吧」 = Let me do it).

Example sentences

妈妈让我早点儿回家。
mā ma ràng wǒ zǎo diǎnr huí jiā。
Mom tells me to come home early.
老师叫我们明天带词典。
lǎo shī jiào wǒ men míng tiān dài cí diǎn。
The teacher told us to bring dictionaries tomorrow.
爸爸不让我玩游戏。
bà ba bú ràng wǒ wán yóu xì。
Dad won't let me play video games.
让我想一想。
ràng wǒ xiǎng yi xiǎng。
Let me think about it.
