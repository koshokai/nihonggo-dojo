「毫无」: not the slightest, no ... at all

HSK 6

毫无 + 二音節名詞（意义／关系／办法／兴趣…）

「毫无」 literally means "not even a tiny bit (「毫」)," that is, "no ... at all," a written-style expression of complete negation. It is often followed by a two-syllable abstract noun such as 「意义・关系・办法・准备」. It's close to English "no ... whatsoever" or "not the slightest ...," and is a formal way of saying 「一点儿也没有」. Since it already contains a negative, note that you don't add 「不」 or 「没」 to it.

×「毫无没有办法」 → 「毫无办法」. Also, a two-syllable word sounds natural after it; ×「毫无钱」 is unnatural (→ 「一点儿钱也没有」).

Example sentences

这样争论下去毫无意义。
zhè yàng zhēng lùn xià qu háo wú yì yì。
Continuing to argue like this is completely pointless.
