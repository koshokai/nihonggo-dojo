Japanese phrase of the day · February 20 · Four-character idiom

馬耳東風

ばじとうふう

in one ear and out the other

From a line by the Chinese poet Li Bai about the east wind blowing past a horse's ear, it means paying no attention to other people's advice. In English you'd say it 'goes in one ear and out the other.'

Example

何度注意しても、弟には馬耳東風だ。
なんどちゅうい しても、 おとうとにわ ばじとうふうだ。
No matter how many times I warn my little brother, it goes in one ear and out the other.
